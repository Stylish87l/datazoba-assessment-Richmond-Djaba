# Data Story Pitch: The Transnational Squads: Mapping Africa's World Cup Diaspora

### 1. Dataset & Provenance
* **Target Registries:** Official FIFA 2026 World Cup Provisional Squad Lists, Historical Dual-Eligibility Player Profiles, and CIES Football Observatory Global Migration Matrices.
* **Data Sourcing & Authentication:** Retrieved directly from open-source registries hosted on Transfermarkt API, public data drops from the CIES Football Observatory, and validated historical verification reports from the Confederation of African Football (CAF).

### 2. The Core Narrative (The Story Inside)
The traditional definition of a football "national team" is fundamentally changing, and contemporary African football is leading the global transformation. Analysis of squad entries for the expanded 48-team 2026 FIFA World Cup reveals an unprecedented trend: approximately 25% of all registered athletes represent a national association different from their country of birth.

While critics often view this trend from a Western perspective—assuming African federations simply import finished talent from European academies—the data points to a deeper history of migration, multi-tier scouting, and fluid transnational identity. 

The data exposes a fascinating variance in strategy across the continent:
* **The Diaspora Networks:** The Democratic Republic of Congo (featuring 20 foreign-born athletes) and Morocco (featuring 19 foreign-born athletes) have developed proactive global identification programs to efficiently integrate players born across France, Belgium, Spain, and the Netherlands.
* **The Homegrown Exception:** South Africa stands out as a distinct statistical anomaly. They are one of the few federations at the tournament entering a squad built entirely with 100% homegrown, domestic-born talent.

This story moves past standard game results to trace how historical migration pathways shape modern athletic competition.

### 3. Visual Strategy & Interaction Mechanics
To break down this complex layout, I propose an interactive scrollytelling ecosystem combining two primary views:
* **The Interactive Chord Mapping Engine:** A web-based flow diagram linking global birth locations to their respective African squad emblems. Users can adjust links to view connections by European birth city or specific African destination country.
* **The Unit Matrix Representative Array:** A responsive grid representing each squad as a collection of modular nodes. Players are color-coded by where they were born, and clicking a node reveals their dual-eligibility timeline and the exact point they committed to their national team.

### 4. Societal Impact & Core Audience
This data story frames sports analytics through a sociological lens. It moves past dry player valuation rankings to show how sports help us understand modern citizenship and family heritage. This deep dive targets cultural readers, global football fans, and modern sociology consumers interested in seeing data brought to life through human stories.
